Eight Walks and Hikes Near Waynesville, NC: Choose Your Day

By Mojo Manor · Updated September 7, 2026

All travel guidesLocal GuidePlan a stay at the Manor ↗

A short trail can still be steep, and a beautiful destination can take longer to reach than a map first suggests. Use these eight options to choose the kind of day you want, then check the current route and conditions before leaving Waynesville.

1. Waterrock Knob: a viewpoint or a climb

NPS describes Waterrock Knob’s summit route as about a mile round trip with roughly 460 feet of climbing. The first paved stretch gives way to uneven dirt and rock. The visitor-area view is a separate option if the climb does not suit everyone. Check access at Parkway milepost 451.2 and bring layers.

2. Richland Balsam: a forest loop

The NPS trail list places the 1.5-mile Richland Balsam loop at milepost 431.0 and rates it moderate. Choose it for time among the trees. The nearby highest-point roadside overlook is a different stop, so navigate to the trailhead you intend to use.

3. Graveyard Fields: choose the route, then the distance

Graveyard Fields has intersecting paths and different waterfall destinations. A loop, Lower Falls visit and route including Upper Falls are not the same walk. Use the trailhead map at Parkway milepost 418.8 and check the NPS trail listing before choosing your turnaround point. Steps and wet ground matter when deciding whether the route suits your group.

4. Max Patch: open landscape with a mountain approach

The Max Patch stewardship guide describes a 1.5-mile loop and a narrow approach road with limited parking. Check current access and posted restrictions before leaving, stay on designated paths and choose another outing if weather or the approach is unsuitable. Allow time for the mountain approach.

5. Looking Glass Rock: allow for a sustained climb

The Forest Service trail guide lists Looking Glass Rock Trail as 3.1 miles one way, making an out-and-back about 6.2 miles. This is a substantial walking commitment. Plan water, food and the return journey, and keep away from exposed rock edges. Looking Glass Falls is a separate roadside attraction, not the destination of this summit trail.

6. Cataloochee: choose a valley walk after checking access

Start with the NPS Cataloochee area guide and current conditions. Its mountain approach deserves time of its own. Use the area map to choose a trail and turnaround that fit your day. Wildlife viewing may be part of a visit, but sightings are not guaranteed.

7. Parkway overlooks: a scenic stop without a summit

A viewpoint can be the right outing for a mixed group. Check the specific stop and open Parkway sections; overlooks differ in surface, parking and facilities. Pick a stop with the facilities and access your group needs.

8. Soco Falls: a short viewing walk

Soco Falls is a viewing stop on the Maggie Valley–Cherokee corridor, rather than a long mountain hike. Our Soco Falls guide covers directions and the maintained viewing route.

Put the day together

Choose one main hike. Add a second stop only if the weather, parking and group’s energy allow it. Save directions for use without a signal, carry what you need for changing conditions and set a turnaround time that leaves room for the drive home. Check the drive to your chosen trailhead when setting your start time.

In the Smokies, check the parking-tag requirements and current trail restrictions. A parking tag does not reserve a space. If bringing a dog, read our dog-friendly guide before choosing a park trail.

Mojo Manor gives you an in-town base one mile from downtown, with a hot tub, game room and shared living spaces for the return. Choose the hike to fit the stay, with time left to enjoy both.
