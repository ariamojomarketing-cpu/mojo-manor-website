Smokies Day Trips from Waynesville: Choose One Part of the Park

By Mojo Manor · Updated September 7, 2026

All travel guidesLocal GuidePlan a stay at the Manor ↗

Great Smoky Mountains National Park is large enough that choosing one part of it is the most useful first step. A Cataloochee visit, an outing through Cherokee and a hike on the Tennessee side are different days. Pick the experience you want before filling in the route.

Cataloochee: a valley visit with a mountain approach

Use the NPS Cataloochee area guide for the Cove Creek Road approach and check current road conditions. Allow time for the narrow mountain drive. Choose a short walk or a historic stop according to what is open, rather than assuming you can travel straight through to the rest of the park.

Bring binoculars if wildlife is part of the appeal, and follow NPS wildlife-viewing guidance. Remain at least 50 yards from elk and bears and farther away if animals react to you. Do not enter a closed meadow or approach to improve a photograph. Sightings depend on the animals, not an arrival-time guarantee.

Cherokee and Oconaluftee: a focused North Carolina day

An outing through Cherokee can focus on the Oconaluftee area without driving all the way across the park. Choose the visitor center, a permitted walk and time for lunch. Decide in advance whether the group wants a valley-level day or a longer mountain drive.

The Museum of the Cherokee People is a separate cultural stop in Cherokee. Check the museum’s hours and your park plan before combining them, so each gets enough time. Pack food and water for the park portion instead of depending on a restaurant along a mountain road.

Newfound Gap and Kuwohi: allow a mountain day

Kuwohi’s observation tower requires a steep paved walk of about half a mile each way from the parking area. Paved does not mean level. Choose the parking-area scenery instead if the climb does not suit your group.

Check current conditions and road access before planning the drive. Visibility may be limited by cloud, and seasonal or weather closures can affect the spur road. Plan the journey as an outing of its own, with time to turn back if conditions change.

Alum Cave: account for the whole hike

NPS lists Alum Cave Bluffs as 4.6 miles round trip. Allow time for both the outward walk and the descent. The trail includes stairs, steep sections and uneven ground, and the popular trailhead can fill.

The trailhead is on the Tennessee side of Newfound Gap Road. Include the drive over the mountains and the return hike before adding anything else. Continuing to Mount Le Conte is a longer, more strenuous undertaking, not an incidental extra after reaching the bluffs.

Parking and access are separate checks

For a normal day visit, the key routine charge is a parking tag for stops longer than 15 minutes. Display the physical tag as directed. A tag does not reserve a parking place. Check current alerts and any requirements specific to your chosen activity as well as arranging parking.

Check park alerts before leaving even when you have a tag. Specific roads, trails, facilities or programs can have their own restrictions or reservations. Keep a second outing in mind if the route or parking you wanted is unavailable.

Save the Gatlinburg side for a deliberate plan

Roaring Fork and other Gatlinburg-side outings add travel beyond the North Carolina entrance. If that is the part of the park you want, build the day around it and check its seasonal road access. Do not treat it as a quick return-route addition to a Cataloochee morning.

Back in Waynesville, Mojo Manor is one mile from downtown, with a stocked kitchen and shared spaces for the evening. View the house film and use the Local Guide to choose another kind of outing for the following day.
