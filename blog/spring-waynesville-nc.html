Spring in Waynesville, NC: What's Actually Worth Doing Right Now

By Mojo Manor  ·  April 2026  ·  5 min read

All travel guidesLocal GuidePlan a stay at the Manor ↗

People talk about fall in Western NC. The leaf peepers, the packed Parkway, the Instagram crowds. What they don't talk about is spring - and that's honestly fine with us, because it keeps things quieter.

Here's what's happening in and around Waynesville right now, and what's worth building a trip around.

Cataloochee Valley: Go Early, Go Weekday

Cataloochee is a 45-minute drive from Waynesville, and in spring it's one of the best places in the Southeast. The elk herd - around 200 animals - has been expanding for years. Calves start appearing in late April and May, which means right now is one of the best windows you'll get.

The valley opens at dawn. We tell every Mojo Manor guest the same thing: get there by 6 AM. Bring binoculars. Park near the old Caldwell House, walk quiet, and give the elk space. You'll almost always find them in the meadows on the north side of the valley road. The light at that hour is good too, if you're there with a camera.

No crowds on weekday mornings. No entry fee. Just drive in, be patient, and let it happen.

Waterfalls Are Running Hard Right Now

Snowmelt plus spring rain means WNC waterfalls hit peak volume in April and early May. Two worth prioritizing this season:

  • Soco Falls (20 min from Mojo Manor) - twin falls on the edge of the Cherokee reservation, free, no permit required. The platform gives you a clean view of both drops. The rope scramble down to the base is worth it in spring when the pool is full - just go slow, the rocks are slippery.
  • Looking Glass Falls (45 min via US-276 near Brevard) - one of the most accessible big waterfalls in the state. You walk 20 feet from the parking lot to a 60-foot drop. It sounds like a tourist trap. It's genuinely impressive.

We've got a full waterfall guide on the blog if you want more options - there are eight good ones within an hour of Waynesville.

The Blue Ridge Parkway Is Fully Open

Sections of the Parkway close in winter. By April they're open again. The stretch starting at Milepost 428.5 (off US-276, about 15 minutes from downtown Waynesville) runs through flowering redbud and dogwood trees in mid-April. It's a 45-minute drive to downtown Asheville if you take the Parkway instead of the highway. Slower, obviously. Worth it at least once.

Pull off at Waterrock Knob (Milepost 451.2) for the ridge views. On a clear spring morning, you can see layers of ridges rolling out toward Tennessee. The summit trail is 0.7 miles round trip - easy enough for most people, steep enough at the end to earn the view.

Downtown Waynesville in April

The Haywood County Farmers Market reopens in spring at the Haywood County Fairgrounds - Saturdays, 8 AM to noon. Local produce is still limited this early in the season, but the vendors are back and it's a good Saturday morning ritual before a hike.

Panacea Coffee on Main Street opens early if you need coffee before anything else. The walk between there and the shops further down Main Street takes about 20 minutes and you'll pass art galleries, a few lunch spots, and Sweet Onion at the end of the block - arguably the best specialty food shop in the county. Good cheese, good charcuterie, good wine if you're stocking the kitchen at Mojo Manor for a longer stay.

One More Thing: Book Before Memorial Day

Spring availability fills up fast, especially Memorial Day weekend (May 23-26). Families who wait until May to start looking usually find the best weekends are gone. If you're targeting late May, the window to book is now.
